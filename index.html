the longer story

Who I am and what I build

I'm Ryan Kevin Omondi Konyango (瑞安), a backend engineer and computer science undergraduate in Hangzhou, originally from Zimbabwe. I design scalable backend architectures, distributed task queues, and resilient REST APIs, and I build them as modular, production-ready systems grounded in clean architecture and solid data flows.

Why I do it

I want to bridge cultures and solve real historical and social data-representation challenges through software. Technology should preserve heritage, streamline human experiences, and empower communities, whether that is a cultural preservation platform or an automated data pipeline.

How I work

I plan visually first, mapping data flows and state interactions before writing backend logic. I pair that with Docker, Redis, Celery, and automated CI/CD so what I ship is maintainable and built to scale.

Where I'm heading

Right now I'm focused on high-impact distributed systems and on expanding my footprint across global tech ecosystems, with the longer-term goal of architecting large-scale backend infrastructure.

Annsana

Digital platform that bridges social expression and archival research, with a focus on preserving and celebrating African cultures.

problem Archives tend to stay static. Heritage needs a platform that preserves cultural data with integrity and speed.

  • 5 Django apps
  • 7 delivery phases shipped
requestClient (HTMX + Tailwind)Django 5 + DRF v1PostgreSQL 16
asyncDjangoRedis brokerCelery workers (Pillow)Processed media
  • Async media pipeline. Image processing runs in Celery workers behind a Redis broker, so uploads return immediately and workers scale independently of the web tier.
  • Versioned, rate-limited API. DRF endpoints secured with OAuth2/JWT, with Redis-backed rate limiting protecting shared routes.
  • Clear boundaries. Designed the PostgreSQL schema and a modular monolith so each domain can evolve in its own app.

Inspiration. History, heritage, and cultural identity deserve robust, modern digital homes. I wanted to build a platform that transcends static archives and actively preserves cultural data with integrity and speed.

Tools. Django 5, PostgreSQL 16, Celery, Redis, Tailwind CSS, Docker, and versioned REST APIs.

Experience. Designing Annsana pushed me to master asynchronous media pipelines and complex relational data modeling. Handling heavy media assets cleanly in the background without blocking the main thread taught me invaluable lessons about real-world system responsiveness and data integrity.

Kenzim

Installable progressive web app for the Kenyan community in Zimbabwe: one platform for membership, communication, events, and a community marketplace, deployable entirely on free-tier cloud infrastructure. Built for a client.

problem A community coordinating through WhatsApp groups and email broadcasts, with manual member registration and verification.

  • design targets
  • <2 s page load on 4G
  • 95%+ availability on free tier
  • 5,000+ members of headroom
appInstallable PWA (Next.js on Vercel)Supabase Auth (role hierarchy)PostgreSQL (members, dues, events)
mapLeafletOpenStreetMap tiles
  • Installable PWA. Installs on mobile and desktop and falls back gracefully when connectivity drops.
  • Secure member management. Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL back a multi-level role hierarchy, with an administrative approval workflow for new members.
  • Community operations. Dues tracking, event registration with RSVP, news publishing, photo galleries, a member directory, a marketplace, and an interactive community map.
  • Cost-efficient by design. Runs entirely on free-tier services (Vercel, Supabase, OpenStreetMap), with a straightforward migration path to paid tiers as the community grows.

source on GitHub live site

full write-up

Inspiration. Born from my role bridging communities between Zimbabwe and Kenya, I needed a centralized space to manage joint initiatives, coordinate leadership efforts, and archive public events effectively.

Tools. Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Supabase (Auth + Postgres), Leaflet with OpenStreetMap, and Vercel, shipped as an installable PWA.

Experience. Kenzim taught me to design around real constraints: role-based access for a multi-tier leadership structure, an admin approval workflow for new members, and a full-featured platform that stays deployable entirely on free-tier infrastructure.

Design targets. Under 2 seconds average page load on 4G, 95%+ availability on free-tier infrastructure, and headroom for 5,000+ members without architectural changes.

Success targets (to be measured after launch). 70%+ member adoption within six months, 80% less manual registration and verification effort, 50% faster event registration, 30% higher event attendance, and 40% more engagement with announcements than WhatsApp or email broadcasts.

Experience. Merging classical backend logic with a machine learning predictive pipeline (achieving 92% accuracy) was a breakthrough moment in understanding how data science and backend engineering feed into each other.
